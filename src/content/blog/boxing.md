If you think about boxing, it's a wierd martial art. In fact, I'm going to go as far as to say that this could be closer classified as a performance than a combat sport. If you want to train martial arts you would want to learn a discipline that includes your hands, feet, striking, & tussling. Boxing is just striking. It's not me trying to diminish it. Trainning is momentously difficult. Pro wrestling has the same characterisitics. Hard training, high risk of injury. More likely to use a boxing move in a street fight than a pro wrestling move. This reminds me of a clip at the Red Lion in Champaign, IL where one kid body slammed another. Crazy Brock Lesnar impersonation. 

MMA, Muay Thai if you're training for a holisitic fighting style. These utilize like 50% more of your body right away, you can start fucking around and finding out what your lower half is capaable of. Although there are scenarios where a legit fight where anything respectable is sanctioned can be 99% boxing, no takedowns, no limbs besides the arms. Same goes for the other end of it, 99% wrestling. 

I did boxing for some years, hard training, but once I tried a different martial art, it clicked why I switched from boxing and madd the right choice. First, the posture, I'm 6'5 and having a hunch or "defensive shell" isn't tall-coded. I've got long ass legs, so let me fucking use them. That boxing stance has residual effects of other aspects of daily life i'm sure of it. Without rambling on for too much, I believe it stiffled my confidence. Muay Thai, straight up, come at me bro, I know I'm dangerous, didn't really feel that in a boxing envirionment.
